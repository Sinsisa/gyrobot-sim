Gyrobot

A small spherical ground drone that folds into a ball when idle — and opens its wheels to drive when it is needed.

Built for site inspection, surveillance and recreation: it can sit unnoticed as a ball, then roll out and explore. This is a browser simulation of the wheel mechanism and its traction.

What it is

Ball when idle

The wheels retract and the body becomes a compact sphere, so it reads as a ball rather than a machine.

Wheels that grow

Segments swing out to enlarge the rolling circle — no claws, no gripping. The tread diameter is the control variable.

Ground drone

Site inspection, surveillance and recreation. Front and rear range sensors keep it clear of walls and furniture.

How it behaves

Closed wheels mean no traction. The chassis rests on the floor and the treads spin 9 mm clear of it — the robot cannot move until the wheels are expanded past the body.
Expanded wheels mean it rolls. Ride height is the larger of the tread radius and the chassis drop, so the body lifts as the tread reaches the ground.
It cannot climb. No steps, no kerbs: flat floors and a flat garden, which is why the house has a flush threshold rather than a doorstep.
It stops short of things. Front and rear readings drive the display; anything under 220 mm turns red.
